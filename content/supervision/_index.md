# Supervision and prospective students

I welcome enquiries from students interested in Palaeolithic archaeology and lithic technology, at undergraduate and graduate level alike. If your interests overlap with the themes below — or sit somewhere adjacent that you think could work — please get in touch.

## Topics I would like to supervise

- **Technological and behavioural variability in the Late Pleistocene** — the Initial Upper Palaeolithic, Ahmarian, Aurignacian, and Gravettian in southern Europe and the Levant, and the Middle and Later Stone Age in southern Africa
- **Cultural transmission, mobility, and human–climate interaction** — what actually drives behavioural change: population movement and contact, environmental forcing, or innovation within local groups, and how we might tell these apart in the archaeological record
- **Quantitative and computational lithic analysis** — attribute analysis, multivariate statistics, and reproducible workflows in R
- **3D scanning and geometric morphometrics** — artefact digitisation protocols, shape analysis, and 3D-based approaches to reduction sequences
- **Experimental archaeology** — knapping experiments designed to test archaeological hypotheses
- **Open science in archaeology** — open data repositories, code sharing, and reproducibility

## Undergraduate and MA dissertations

Dissertation projects can draw on the Department's experimental facilities and dedicated lithics laboratories, as well as on the open datasets and 3D models I have published through the [**Open Aurignacian Project**](https://www.armandofalcucci.com/project/open_aurignacian/) and my [**datasets page**](https://www.armandofalcucci.com/datasets). These are a good starting point for a project that draws on some of the most important Palaeolithic sites excavated across Italy and the Levant (Grotta della Cala, Grotta di Castelcivita, Grotta di Fumane, Ksar Akil, Riparo Bombrini).

## PhD applicants

I am currently able to consider new doctoral students.

Doctoral research is hosted by the [**Department of Archaeology**](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/study/postgraduate-research/archaeology), one of the largest archaeology research programmes in the UK, with purpose-built laboratories for lithic analysis and Palaeolithic archaeology. Within the department I am a member of the [**Centre for the Archaeology of Human Origins (CAHO)**](https://caho.soton.ac.uk/), which brings together researchers working on human origins and makes co-supervision across complementary specialisms straightforward.

There are two routes in: propose your own project, or apply to an advertised studentship. Funded studentships run in partnership with the [**South, West and Wales Doctoral Training Partnership**](https://www.sww-ahdtp.ac.uk/). Departmental scholarships are usually advertised in November or December, with deadlines the following February or March. PhDs start in September, and applications for September entry close on 1 July.

Full details on entry requirements, fees, and how to apply are on the [Archaeology postgraduate research pages](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/study/postgraduate-research/archaeology). Please get in touch well ahead of these deadlines — competitive applications take months, not weeks, and I would rather develop a proposal with you than receive a finished one.

## Early-career researchers

Drawing on my own experience as a Global Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellow, I am happy to mentor postdoctoral candidates preparing applications for **Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions** and comparable fellowship schemes, including hosting arrangements at the University of Southampton.

[**Send me an email**](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/people/676smr/doctor-armando-falcucci) with:
